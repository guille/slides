A short history of the Philippines

Kasaysayan

From the barangay to the republic

Kasaysayan is Tagalog for history. Its root, saysay, means both "story" and "significance": the historian Zeus Salazar defined history as salaysay na may saysay, a story that has meaning.

An archipelago, not a single place

Three island groups, Luzon, the Visayas and Mindanao, are the three stars on the flag.

The island count went from 7,107 to 7,641 in 2016, when the national mapping agency (NAMRIA) re-surveyed the coastline with better imagery.

2020 census: 109,035,343. Languages: Ethnologue lists about 175 living languages. Filipino (based on Tagalog) and English are official.

A thousand years in eight moments

The oldest dated document found in the islands: a debt pardon in Old Malay. Lapulapu's warriors kill Magellan on the beach at Mactan, 27 April. 333 years of Spanish rule begin. Manila becomes the capital in 1571. Three secular priests are garroted after the Cavite mutiny. The Katipunan rises. Rizal is shot in Manila on 30 December. Declared at Kawit on 12 June. In December, Spain cedes the islands to the US. After American rule and Japanese occupation, independence on 4 July. Four days on EDSA end Ferdinand Marcos's twenty years in power.

Part 1 · before 1565

Barangays and sultanates

Islands connected by the sea, not divided by it

The barangay

Named after the balangay, the plank-built boats that carried settlers and traders between islands.

  • Datu: the chief, by birth and by deeds
  • Maharlika: warrior nobility
  • Timawa: free commoners
  • Alipin: dependents, often in debt bondage

Beyond the village

  • Polities on rivers and bays: Tondo, Butuan, Cebu
  • Trade with China, Brunei, Java and Champa
  • Islam in the south: the Sulu sultanate by the 15th century, Maguindanao by the 16th

A barangay was typically 30 to 100 households. Classes follow Tagalog terms; the Visayan equivalents differ.

Boats excavated at Butuan from 1976 on are among the oldest watercraft excavated in Southeast Asia.

Baybayin: try writing a word

An abugida: each sign is a consonant plus "a"; a mark above (kudlit) makes it e/i, below makes it o/u. Final consonants were simply not written.

The Spanish printed it in the Doctrina Christiana (Manila, 1593). Francisco López added the cross-shaped virama in 1620 so final consonants could be written.

Part 2 · 1565–1898

Three centuries of Spain

Manila becomes a hinge between Asia and the Americas

The Manila galleon

In 1565 Andrés de Urdaneta found the way back east: sail north with the Kuroshio current to around 40°N, then ride the westerlies to California and follow the coast south.

Silver from Mexico and Peru paid for Chinese goods in Manila. The Mexican War of Independence ended the trade; the last galleon sailed in 1815.

Rizal's novels Noli me tángere (1887) and El filibusterismo (1891) indicted the friar-run colonial order. He argued for reform, not revolution, but was executed as its instigator.

Part 3 · 1896 onward

Revolution and republic

Independence, twice

Two independence days

12 June 1898

  • Emilio Aguinaldo declares independence at Kawit, Cavite
  • The flag is unfurled and the national march first played
  • The US does not recognize it; war follows, 1899–1902

4 July 1946

  • The Treaty of Manila: the US recognizes the Republic
  • Manuel Roxas is its first president

1962: President Diosdado Macapagal moves Independence Day to 12 June

4 July is still observed, as Philippine–American Friendship Day.

The Malolos Republic (1899) was the first constitutional republic in Asia.

People Power, 1986

  • 1972: Ferdinand Marcos declares martial law. It is lifted in 1981; his rule is not
  • 1983: opposition leader Ninoy Aquino is shot dead at Manila airport, returning from exile
  • February 1986: a fraud-ridden snap election, then a military revolt
  • 22–25 February: crowds fill EDSA to shield the rebels. Marcos flies to Hawaii; Corazon Aquino takes office

EDSA is Epifanio de los Santos Avenue, the avenue between the two military camps the rebels held. Hundreds of thousands stood in the way of the tanks, with nuns and priests in front.
